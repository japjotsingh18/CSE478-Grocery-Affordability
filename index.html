Can Students Still Afford the Basics?

An Interactive Visual Story of Food Prices and the Student Grocery Budget

This project explores how grocery prices in the U.S. have changed from 2016 - 2026 as well as how these changes can affect college students. We are starting by comparing the prices of 3 common grocery items:- eggs, milk, and bread.

Japjot Singh · Ramneek Kaur · Anushvi Arora

Grocery Prices in the U.S. (2016–2026)

This line chart shows how the prices of eggs, milk and bread have changed between 2016 and 2026. It helps us identify long-term price trends and periods when grocery prices increased or decreased significantly.

Select the grocery items you want to see. Hover over the lines to view their prices.

Legend and product selector

Milk and bread prices have generally increased over time, while egg prices have experienced more noticeable fluctuations. Egg prices increased sharply during late 2022 and early 2025 before decreasing again. These changes can make it difficult for students to plan a consistent grocery budget.

Note: October 2025 data is unavailable.

Note: Eggs are priced per dozen, milk per gallon and bread per pound. Each item should be compared with its own past prices.

Monthly Grocery Price Heatmap (2016-2026)

This heatmap illustrates how the monthly costs of bread, milk as well as eggs has changed over time. Hover your cursor over a cell to view the item's pricing. Select an item from the dropdown menu and then compare the colors over months and years.

Select a grocery item. Hover over a cell to view its monthly price.

Missing observation Outside dataset range

According to the heatmap, egg prices remained comparatively stable from 2016 to 2021 before becoming more uneven from 2022 onward. In late 2022 and early 2023, prices increased dramatically; later in 2023, they decreased; and in 2024 and early 2025, they increased once again. The hatched November and December 2026 cells are outside the dataset period, while the gray cell indicates an unavailable Oct.2025 observation. These monthly variations show why examining the full timeline provides context for the project's inquiry regarding grocery costs and student spending plans.

Grocery Price Growth vs. Volatility

This scatterplot compares the monthly volatility and price increase for bread, milk as well as eggs over a chosen range. Select the beginning and ending years to observe how much each item's price fluctuates over that time.

Choose a year range. Hover over or focus on a point to view its price statistics.

The chart shows milk and bread with greater price growth and lower volatility than eggs for 2016–2026. Bread has the highest growth, while milk and bread have similar volatility. Eggs show little overall growth but much greater month-month variation. When taken as a whole, the points indicate that these three food products have different price increase and volatility.

Grocery Basket Cost Breakdown

This treemap visualizes the cost breakdown of an illustrative grocery basket over time. Each rectangle represents a different food item, with the size of the rectangle indicating its proportion of the total basket cost.

Illustrative basket · 1 dozen eggs, 1 gallon milk, 1 pound bread

Total basket cost

In October 2016, the illustrative basket cost $6.025; by October 2026, it cost $8.282, an increase of about 37%. Milk remained the largest part of the basket in both years, though its share fell from 54.64% to 49.52%. Eggs made up a larger share in 2026, while bread's share stayed close to 22%.

About the Data

  • Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), accessed through FRED.
  • Items: Eggs, milk, and white bread.
  • Time Period: January 2016 – October 2026.
  • Dataset: 387 monthly price records.
  • Missing Data: October 2025 prices were unavailable, so those values were left out.
Data Sources (FRED, from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics): Eggs (APU0000708111) · Milk (APU0000709112) · Bread (APU0000702111)
